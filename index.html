Why it works

Built around the decision
a customer actually makes.

Most small-business sites lose people in the first few seconds. These are the four things worth fixing first.

01 / First impression

People decide fast

Your name, what you do and where you work should be clear before anyone scrolls. No guessing, no hunting through a slideshow.

02 / Phones first

Most visits are mobile

Layouts are designed on a phone screen first and then opened up for desktop, rather than squeezed down afterwards.

03 / The next step

One obvious action

Call, message or request a quote. Every section points to the single step you actually want a visitor to take.

04 / Speed & trust

Fast pages, real details

Lightweight code, compressed images, honest hours and a real address. Slow or vague pages quietly cost you calls.

Before we talk

Questions, answered.

Are the demos real client projects?

No. All eight are fictional design concepts. Every business name, price, address, phone number and review inside them is invented, and the imagery is illustrative. They demonstrate design and structure, not client results.

Can I just use one of the themes as-is?

A theme is a starting point for layout, typography and tone — not a finished site. Your copy, photos, services, colours and any extra pages get built on top of it. Naming the one you liked simply gives the first conversation a direction.

What will my website cost?

Send a short description of what you need. You will get the work outlined and a written quote including any ongoing costs before you decide. Nothing is charged for the conversation or the proposal.

Does “NextDay” mean it launches tomorrow?

No, it is the brand name. Your proposal sets a realistic timeline based on scope, content and revisions. The usual hold-up is content, not code.

What if I do not have good photos?

Most businesses do not at first. Your own phone photos are often better than stock, and the proposal will say plainly whether photography is included, licensed or something you will supply later.

Will I own the website?

The proposal specifies the files, accounts and access included in the handoff, along with any third-party licences. Domain and service accounts should be in your name from day one.

Who updates it after launch?

Decided in the proposal. Some clients want written instructions and handle small edits themselves; others prefer to send changes over. Either way the cost is agreed up front rather than billed by surprise.

Can you work with my existing site?

Yes. Send the link and what you would like to improve, and you will get an honest assessment of whether to update it or rebuild.
